04 · Memory Aids

Write a BRIEF. Follow the DVD.

Prompting is briefing — so write a BRIEF. The five letters are a checklist, not a script: cover all five in any order.

B
Background
The context. Paste the data, the history, the prior emails.
R
Role
Who the AI should be. "You are a communications director…"
I
Intent
The mission — the specific deliverable and its audience.
E
Expectations
The constraints — length, tone, format, what to avoid.
F
Format & standard
What excellent looks like — an example, or the criteria you'll judge by.

Your one-page AI policy has three rules — the DVD Rule. Dated like the disc; impossible to forget.

D
Data
Know what never goes in: client PII, employee records, financials under NDA, credentials. Exposed credentials get rotated immediately.
V
Verify
AI drafts, humans sign. Names, numbers, dates, and citations get independently checked before anything ships.
D
Disclose
Decide once, in advance, where you disclose AI assistance — and write the decision down.
05 · Field Discipline

DOs & DON'Ts of Using AI

✓ DO

  • Treat the first output as a staff estimate — iterate in the same conversation instead of restarting.
  • Paste context generously. The model only knows what it was trained on plus what you give it.
  • Make it interrogate you: "Before drafting, ask me the five questions you need answered."
  • Use it for T-tasks — drafts, summaries, options, reformatting — where unverified output is tolerable.
  • Verify names, numbers, dates, and citations independently, every time, before anything ships.
  • Master one assistant deeply before adding any other tool.
  • Capture a baseline before rollout so progress claims are credible.
  • Tell your team what you use it for. Modeled behavior beats mandated behavior.

✗ DON'T

  • Don't paste client PII, employee records, NDA material, or credentials into consumer AI tools. Ever.
  • Don't ship AI output unreviewed. Accountability is not transferable — "the AI suggested it" survives nothing.
  • Don't trust confident citations, statistics, or legal/medical claims without checking. Confidence is not accuracy.
  • Don't buy six tools to feel like you're adopting. Subscriptions are easy to start and invisible to carry.
  • Don't use it as a search engine and conclude it's overhyped. Shallow usage produces shallow results.
  • Don't mandate by ambush — a tool announced Monday, mandatory Friday, builds resistance, not adoption.
  • Don't investigate an exposed API key or password. Rotate it immediately; investigate after.
  • Don't let the tool decide. It generates options; you own outcomes.
